My research sits at the intersection of Operations Research and Machine Learning. I develop decision support methods for planning problems in which uncertainty matters, and apply them in health care, logistics and environmental decision-making. A common methodological thread is the use of structured models, such as networks and decision diagrams, that are expressive enough for real-world problems and at the same time amenable to learning. Many papers belong to more than one of the lines below; papers are listed newest first. For funded projects, see Projects.

Decisions under uncertainty

Many planning decisions have to be made before demand, arrivals or durations are known. I work on two-stage, robust and data-driven approaches that account for this uncertainty, on quantifying the value of information, and on decision-focused learning, where predictive models are trained with respect to the decisions they inform.

Network models and decision diagrams

State-expanded networks encode complex rules, for example for shifts and rosters, in the structure of a graph, so that the remaining optimization model stays simple. Decision diagrams provide relaxations and bounds for discrete optimization. My work develops such models and uses machine learning to make them smaller and their bounds stronger.

Health care analytics

Hospitals have to plan beds, staff and treatments while patient arrivals and lengths of stay are uncertain. Together with hospital partners and colleagues from medicine, I develop simulation-, learning- and optimization-based decision support for such problems: forecasting and managing intensive care occupancy, scheduling ICU admissions, scheduling clinical pathways, and rostering staff. I have also worked with clinicians and computer scientists on algorithm-based rehabilitation after cochlear implantation.

Language models for optimization

Formulating an optimization model is often the bottleneck for using optimization in practice. In the project OptimizationChat we investigate how large language models can help to formalize and solve industrial planning problems through natural-language interaction. Related work uses language models to discover heuristics for combinatorial optimization.

  • Hottung, Berto, Hua, …, Römer, …, Tierney (2026). VRPAgent: LLM-Driven Discovery of Heuristic Operators for Vehicle Routing Problems. Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR). arXiv

Environment and sustainability

Environmental decisions, such as where to monitor, what to protect and how to weigh ecological against economic goals, are made under substantial uncertainty and with limited budgets. Together with environmental economists and marine biodiversity researchers, I study the value of information in environmental monitoring, trade-off analysis for environmental decisions, and, in a DFG project, decision-focused learning for planning marine protected areas.

Logistics and supply chains

Many of my projects start from operational problems in logistics and retail: service network design, inventory routing, storage assignment and inventory management in e-grocery fulfilment, truck loading, and most recently large-scale supply network optimization in the automotive industry. Logistics also extends into space: a time-space network approach for delivery scheduling won first place in the “Delivery Scheduling” category of the ESA/GECCO Space Optimisation Competition 2022, and our exact framework for the space-time dependent TSP (INFORMS Journal on Computing) solves asteroid routing problems, in which a spacecraft visits a sequence of asteroids (Space.com).
